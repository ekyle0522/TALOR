Tail Cues, Principal Corrections: Plug-and-Play Rectification for Open-Set Test-Time Adaptation

Yingkai Yang·Chaoqi Chen·Hui Huang

Shenzhen University

Paper arXiv Code Dataset DOCO BibTeX

TALOR (Test-time Adaptation via Linear Online Rectification) is a small, backward-free module that sits between the feature extractor and the frozen linear head. It uses the head's own SVD to split every feature into high-gain principal coordinates and low-gain tail coordinates, predicts from the tail how the distribution shift has moved the principal coordinates, and subtracts that drift before the unchanged head. It needs no backpropagation, no source data and no source statistics. It works on a frozen model and on top of other TTA methods.

Method

How TALOR works

TALOR is a gradient-free layer between the feature extractor and the source linear head. The backbone and the head stay unchanged.

1

Head-induced coordinates

The SVD of the frozen head, W = UΣV⊤, splits the normalized feature into principal coordinates (the top singular directions holding τ = 80 % of the spectral energy) and tail coordinates. On ViT-B/16 this gives 353 principal and 415 tail dimensions.

2

Tail cues

Tail coordinates barely change the logits, but they respond to the shift, and their drift linearly predicts part of the principal drift (tail-to-principal leakage, see below).

3

Soft-routed affine drift

A two-component GMM on the tail energy ‖u‖² gives soft ID / OOD responsibilities. For each branch TALOR keeps a principal mean and a tail-to-principal slope, fitted online by weighted ridge regression and smoothed with an EMA. The ridge problem is solved in its dual form, so its cost depends on the batch size rather than on the tail dimension.

4

Rectified inference

The predicted drift is subtracted from the principal coordinates only. The feature is rebuilt with the untouched tail and the original norm, then passed to the unchanged head.

Where the head is cut

TALOR keeps the top singular directions of the frozen head that hold a fraction τ of its squared singular values. Drag τ to move the cut.

τ = 0.80 → 353 principal · 415 tail

Why tail cues?

We compare the features of clean ImageNet images and their corrupted copies on a frozen ViT-B/16, in the basis induced by the classifier head. Drift in the principal coordinates accounts for most of the change in the logits (left). The tail coordinates barely reach the logits, yet their drift is about as large as their clean variation (middle), and it linearly predicts the principal drift far better than shuffled pairs do (right). TALOR therefore reads the shift from the tail and corrects only the principal coordinates. ResNet-50 shows the same pattern (see the paper's appendix).
